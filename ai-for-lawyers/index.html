AI for Lawyers

Your value lies in judgment, accuracy and accountability to clients. AI can speed up preparation, but it can produce convincing errors, so verification is not optional.

Regulated field. Confidentiality and professional duties come first. Check your employer and regulator rules before using AI with client information. This page is general education, not professional advice, and should be reviewed by a practising lawyer before launch.

What you do today

  • Reviewing and drafting documents
  • Researching law and precedent
  • Explaining matters to clients
  • Managing matters and deadlines

Where AI can help

  • Summarise long documents to decide what needs close reading
  • Draft plain-language explanations for clients
  • Structure a research question into an issue list
  • Produce a first-pass review checklist for a type of contract

Where not to trust it

  • Case citations, statutes and quotations: AI can invent plausible ones
  • Jurisdiction-specific rules unless you have checked them
  • Anything final without your own review
  • Privileged or client-confidential material in tools not approved by your firm and regulator

Example workflow

TaskExplain a contract clause to a client
AI applicationAI drafts a plain-language explanation from the clause text, with identifying details removed
Human verificationConfirm it matches the clause exactly, check the applicable law, and apply your judgment before anything is sent

Example prompts

Explain the clause below in plain language for a non-lawyer in under 150 words. Do not add terms that are not in the text: [clause, identifying details removed].

List the issues a reviewer should check in a [type of contract] as a checklist. Mark which items depend on jurisdiction.

Summarise this document in 10 bullets and quote the passage that supports each point: [text].

Replace the bracketed parts. Never paste confidential information into a tool your organisation has not approved.

Kinds of tools

  • A firm-approved AI assistant
  • AI features in legal research platforms, always checked against primary sources
  • Document review tools, within your firm's data policy

We describe categories rather than endorse products. Tool recommendations will be added once reviewed.

How to verify

  • Look up every citation in a primary source
  • Check quotations against the original text
  • Confirm the law applies in the relevant jurisdiction and date
  • Follow your regulator's guidance on AI and client confidentiality
