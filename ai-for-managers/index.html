AI for Managers

You already know how to run a team and make decisions with incomplete information. Here is where AI can take on preparation work, and where your judgment must stay in charge.

What you do today

  • Weekly reporting and status updates
  • Preparing briefings and meeting notes
  • Making decisions with incomplete information
  • Communicating up, down and across the organisation

Where AI can help

  • Draft the first version of a report from your own notes
  • Summarise long documents or threads before a meeting
  • Compare options and list the assumptions and risks behind each
  • Rewrite a message for a different audience

Where not to trust it

  • Figures, names and facts it states without a source
  • People decisions such as hiring or performance: AI can structure your thinking, not judge people
  • Anything confidential, if your organisation hasn't approved the tool

Example workflow

TaskPrepare a weekly report from raw notes
AI applicationAI organises the notes, flags patterns and drafts a structure
Human verificationCheck every number against its source, remove anything you can't confirm, then add your own judgment

Example prompts

Here are my notes from this week: [paste]. Organise them under progress, risks and decisions needed. Do not add anything that is not in the notes.

I must decide between [option A] and [option B]. List the assumptions each depends on and what could go wrong. Ask me questions if information is missing.

Rewrite this message for [audience] in under 120 words. Keep every commitment unchanged: [paste].

Replace the bracketed parts. Never paste confidential information into a tool your organisation has not approved.

Kinds of tools

  • A general-purpose AI assistant for drafting and summarising
  • AI features inside the email, document and meeting tools you already use
  • Meeting transcription, only where your organisation's policy allows it

We describe categories rather than endorse products. Tool recommendations will be added once reviewed.

How to verify

  • Compare every figure with its original source
  • If it cites a source, open it and confirm it says what is claimed
  • Read for statements you did not provide
  • Confirm tone suits the recipient before sending
