About DiMe

AI shouldn't make experienced professionals feel inexperienced.

DiMe exists to help people who already know how to work understand how AI can work with them.

The problem we saw

Many capable professionals are being told, in effect, to start again. The explanations on offer are written either for engineers or as hype, and neither begins with the work they actually do.

The result isn't a lack of ability. It's a gap between deep professional experience and a clear, relevant way into AI.

Why DiMe exists

DiMe began when its creator, after teaching himself to prompt and work with AI, realised how many people struggle to see how it applies to their own jobs. The missing piece wasn't intelligence. It was explanation that starts from the work.

You don't need to become an AI expert. You need to understand how AI can work with the expertise you already have.

What we believe

Start with your work, not the technology. We begin with your tasks and ask where AI might help, rather than introducing tools and hunting for uses.

Professional judgment is the point. AI can prepare a first draft, organise information and suggest options. It can also be confidently wrong. Deciding what is true, appropriate and acceptable remains with you, and so does accountability.

Honesty about limits. We say what AI can't reliably do, what to verify, and when not to use it, including around confidentiality, bias and regulated work.

The DiMe Method

  1. Understand
  2. Identify
  3. Apply
  4. Verify
  5. Integrate

Every guide, lesson and workflow follows this sequence, and verification is a step, not a footnote.

Who is behind DiMe

Otieno Koduong'o

I don't come from a technology background. My working life has been in hands-on, manual jobs rather than one trade, so I learned AI from the start, without a technical foundation.

I'm a practitioner who tests. I try AI on real tasks, check what holds up, and say plainly what needs verifying. I'm not an AI authority, and DiMe doesn't pretend otherwise. I'm currently building an AI intelligence brief generator that turns scattered information into material for commercial decisions.

In my own testing, AI struggled to separate fact from non-fact and to put information in context. That is why verification is built into everything DiMe teaches.
